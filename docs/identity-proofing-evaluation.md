# Open-source identity proofing and KYC/KYB evaluation

Reviewed October 31, 2026. This is a source and licensing evaluation, not an installed integration, procurement decision, biometric assurance assessment or production approval. The owner confirmed that no approved identity-reproofing provider/process currently exists.

## Decision

Keep identity proofing and KYC/KYB evidence in an OpenPlatform service with explicit provider adapters. Firefly Identity should consume a short-lived, purpose-bound recovery authorization from that owner. Jafly keeps provider-neutral security abstractions and must not acquire dependencies on these products or Firefly Identity.

There are useful open-source components, but this review has not qualified an off-the-shelf, fully open-source worldwide KYC/KYB and biometric recovery stack for Keycloak 26.8.1. The most credible open-source platform candidates in this selection are **MOSIP/eSignet** and **Inji**, where a trusted identity/credential source exists. **OpenBiometrics** is a technical laboratory candidate, with separate model-license and assurance work. No candidate is enabled as a recovery authority.

## Different capabilities

| Capability | Evidence it can supply | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Document verification | Authenticity checks and extracted identity data; chip/issuer signature checks where supported | That the person presenting it owns the existing bank account |
| Face comparison and liveness | A live capture matching an approved reference under a defined assurance policy | Document authenticity, account ownership, independent possession or a recovery permission |
| KYC/KYB orchestration | Cases, evidence collection, review, decisions and provider integration | A trustworthy document/biometric verification engine or authoritative company data |
| Screening | Potential sanctions/PEP/adverse-information matches | Identity authentication, business ownership or authorization to unlock |
| Verifiable credentials/OIDC | Signed claims from a selected trusted issuer | That the issuer performed acceptable proofing or that the claims authorize recovery |

The license of an orchestration application does not license its models, weights, document templates, datasets, register access or hosted API. Record each separately. For example, [OpenSanctions states separate commercial dataset terms](https://www.opensanctions.org/docs/commercial/exemption/). Software screening code is not an unrestricted sanctions/PEP dataset. KYB also needs approved company-register, beneficial-owner and representative evidence for the relevant jurisdictions; an LEI, company-name match or registry status alone cannot establish the operator's authority.

Projects called “OpenKYC” are not interchangeable. [OpenKYC.org currently says its implementation is private and opening is planned](https://openkyc.org/open-source). [FaceOnLive/OpenKYC redirects to ID-Verification-OpenKYC](https://github.com/FaceOnLive/ID-Verification-OpenKYC), whose reviewed repository has no detected license and advertises a paid SDK. Neither was accepted as a complete reusable open-source stack.

## Keycloak extension findings

The [community BioID extension](https://github.com/danielchristianschroeter/bioid-face-recognition-keycloak) is an actual facial authentication/liveness SPI, but not a complete document-reproofing service. Its reviewed POM pins Keycloak 26.5.6, Compose uses 26.4.7, and packaging skips tests; no 26.8.1 qualification was found. LICENSE is MIT while README claims Apache-2.0. This third-party plugin is not adopted.

[Didit's Keycloak article](https://didit.me/blog/federated-credentials-didit-keycloak/) describes external document/liveness/face-match integration through custom flows, APIs and webhooks. It does not establish a maintained open-source drop-in provider. [Matrix42 Secure Access's Signicat integration](https://docs.efecte.com/en_US/release_note_26_2/secure-access262-release-notes) is a concrete commercial Keycloak identity-proofing integration, with no public reusable OSS plugin or exact upstream 26.8.1 compatibility established here.

WebAuthn/device biometrics authenticate an enrolled authenticator. A face SDK or OIDC broker is not inherently a document-proofing authority. In particular, [iProov's OIDC documentation](https://docs.iproov.com/workforce-mfa/web-client) distinguishes liveness-only from matching an enrolled reference and describes first-visit enrollment. An arbitrary live face or enrollment during account recovery cannot prove ownership of the account.

## Required integration boundary

1. Create an unpredictable reproofing case bound to the existing issuer, tenant, customer identity, recovery purpose and current lock generation. Generic public initiation must not disclose whether the account exists. Do not create or auto-link a customer by email/name equality.
2. The proofing owner selects the approved evidence policy: document/issuer authenticity, live capture, comparison to an appropriately established reference, fraud checks and independent evidence where required. Raw images, biometric templates and document data stay in that evidence boundary with explicit retention/access controls, outside Keycloak attributes and application JWTs.
3. Validate provider callbacks/results on the server: cryptographic authentication, exact case/session/nonce/subject binding, freshness, duplicate/replay control, current status and required evidence completion. Browser redirects or a bare `verified=true` are not authority. Failed or unavailable required checks must refuse or enter separately controlled review.
4. The owner issues a short-lived, single-use authorization for **credential replacement and the eligible lock transition**. It preserves fraud/administrative/employment holds and existing evidence. Every receiving service verifies caller identity and current purpose/resource policy.
5. The native executor requires the exact new purpose, credential revision, approved evidence and current generation. Ordinary authentication stays blocked or restricted to the approved recovery purpose until required replacement factors are bound and superseded, lost or compromised authenticators are retired. Those are eligibility conditions for activation; retiring sessions alone does not retire an old TOTP/passkey. Replacement, the eligible lock transition, durable execution recording and old-session retirement need an authoritative atomic or durably gated protocol with no enablement gap. Do not reuse the current known-password attestation or perform public reset/enable calls as uncoordinated steps.
6. After those activation conditions hold, notify through approved previously verified channels and require fresh ordinary sign-in with the replacement factors. Workforce uses its separate identity/employment owner. Administrator recovery stays separate.

Existing OpenPlatform KYC CRUD/status/saga APIs do not currently supply this fresh recovery authorization. Adopting an external engine does not create that owner contract. European deployment assessment must cover the actual bank, jurisdictions, process and data handling; the [EBA remote-onboarding guidelines](https://www.eba.europa.eu/legacy/regulation-and-policy/regulatory-activities/anti-money-laundering-and-countering-financing-4) provide relevant onboarding guidance, not automatic recovery certification.

## Qualification before selecting a component

The concrete evaluation should pin source, license, model hashes and all data terms; establish supported documents/countries and issuer trust; test genuine vs forged/replayed/injected media with independently justified thresholds; measure accessibility and rejection/review paths; and rehearse failure, revocation, callback substitution, expiry, evidence retention and operator audit. A self-hosted demo, accuracy claim, passing OCR or configurable score is insufficient acceptance for bank recovery.

No candidate software, models or datasets were installed for this evaluation, no vendor accounts were created, and no identity/biometric data were submitted. Candidate adoption and real-provider acceptance remain open. See [access recovery](access-recovery.md) for the implemented known-password unlock and the distinct missing recovery journey.
