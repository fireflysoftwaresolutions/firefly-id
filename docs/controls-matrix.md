# EU and US applicability and controls

**Reviewed November 1, 2026.** This matrix connects selected primary sources to Firefly Identity's implementation, evidence and deployment responsibilities. It is an engineering review aid for EU and US regulated financial entities. It is not a legal opinion, certification, supervisory acceptance or authorization to enable production recovery.

The repository implements a Keycloak distribution and optional native recovery extensions. The regulated entity remains responsible for its applicable obligations and outsourced services. Select controls for the actual entity, jurisdiction, population and journey; a password policy or passing browser test cannot establish the institution's compliance program.

Read the [security boundaries](security.md), [authentication policies](authentication-policies.md), [credential-loss recovery](credential-loss-recovery.md) and [current implementation evidence](progress.md) alongside this page.

## How to use the matrix

Record the applicability decision before selecting a deployment profile. Assign named accountable owners to the functional roles below. Attach evidence to the exact service, configuration, source revision and environment reviewed. A source requirement, implementation and successful test are separate records.

| Classification | Meaning in this page |
| --- | --- |
| Binding law or regulatory standard | An obligation only where its legal scope, effective date and relevant conditions apply. Directly applicable EU regulations differ from directives implemented through national law. |
| Supervisory guidance | Regulator-issued risk-management expectations or interpretation; distinguish these from a new statutory requirement. |
| Technical guidance or standard | A benchmark that may be adopted by policy, contract or a specific legal requirement; its publication alone does not make it law for every bank. |
| Configured | A setting or control exists in this repository; this is not proof of all deployed paths. |
| Qualified synthetic scope | A linked receipt records execution with specified synthetic identities, dependencies and sources. |
| Deployment gate | A required decision, integration, operating procedure or acceptance result remains outside that qualified scope. |

### Applicability worksheet

Complete this worksheet for each deployment and material change. Store the approved record in the institution's controlled evidence system, without placing customer records or secrets in this public documentation.

| Decision | Required record | Accountable owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal entity and activity | Entity, license/charter, home/host jurisdiction, services, branches and primary/secondary supervisors | Legal and compliance |
| Applicable sources | Binding instruments, national implementation, effective dates, exemptions and documented rationale | Legal and compliance |
| Population and access | Customers, workforce, institutional humans, representatives, privileged administrators and workloads; actual resources accessed | Security and business owner |
| Journey | Ordinary sign-in, payment-account access, payment authorization, account maintenance, known-password unlock, credential replacement or representative action | Product and fraud owner |
| Information and locations | Personal/nonpublic data, evidence, credential material, subprocessors, processing/storage/support countries and transfers | Privacy and data owner |
| Required assurance | Identity-proofing and authentication targets, accepted scheme/provider evidence, freshness, factor binding and fallback procedure | Identity assurance and security |
| Authority | Existing customer/party linkage, tenant membership, legal-person mandate, employment and financial permissions, with their authoritative owners | Core/Domain and authorization owner |
| Policy exceptions | Exact exception, legal basis if applicable, compensating controls, approving authority, review and expiry | Compliance and security |
| Evidence and acceptance | Exact installed artifacts/configuration, positive and negative tests, real-provider acceptance, operations exercises and residual risks | Release, operations and independent assurance |

EU applicability must distinguish payment rules from general ICT resilience and privacy obligations. US applicability must distinguish bank-agency rules, FTC jurisdiction and state or sector overlays. Customer nationality, a hostname or a claimed vendor certification alone is not a sufficient applicability decision.

## Actual repository baseline

The [policy catalogue](../config/authentication-policies.json) is the source of these values. They are defaults for isolated synthetic realms. Existing realms require reviewed migration and effective-policy readback; import does not overwrite retained realms.

| Population | Password and session defaults | MFA boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer humans | 16–128 characters; previous five excluded; 300-second SSO idle and access-token lifetime | Optional in the ordinary synthetic profile. The enrolled-possession fixture used by native unlock does not make customer MFA universally required. |
| Workforce humans | 20–128 characters; previous ten excluded; 900-second SSO idle; 300-second access tokens | Browser password and OTP required; TOTP is the sandbox factor. |
| Institutional humans | 16–128 characters; previous five excluded; 300-second SSO idle and access-token lifetime | Optional in the synthetic profile; no organizational mandate or transaction authority inferred. |
| Administrators | 20–128 characters; previous ten excluded; 300-second idle, 3,600-second maximum and 60-second access tokens | Catalogue intent requires MFA; temporary bootstrap access does not enforce or qualify permanent named administrators. Phishing-resistant MFA and private administrative ingress remain gates. |

All populations select Argon2, reject whole passwords equal to username/email, use a small static denylist, and permanently block after the third applicable failed authentication attempt. There is no automatic timed release. The baseline denylist is not comprehensive compromised-password coverage. No arbitrary periodic password expiry is selected. TOTP is not phishing resistant. The ordinary application-realm maximum is 28,800 seconds; none of these numbers is a universal banking-law recipe.

Identity SSO activity, application activity and payer activity are different measurements. Refresh traffic can keep an identity session active while the person is idle. Likewise, disabling an identity or retiring Keycloak sessions does not make every previously issued self-contained JWT immediately unusable at an independent receiver. See [receiver enforcement](zero-trust.md).

Track subsequent payment-law changes and their actual application dates; this page does not assume a successor proposal has displaced the cited operative controls.

### NIST technical assurance benchmark

[Final NIST SP 800-63B-4, July 2025](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/b/4/final) is a technical standard/guideline, with a government-system focus. It becomes an institution-specific requirement through an applicable mandate, contract or adopted policy; it is not a general private-bank MFA statute.

| Topic | Benchmark and actual repository boundary | Owner/evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passwords | NIST requires 15-character single-factor passwords, at least eight within MFA, full-value verification, and a common/expected/compromised-value blocklist. A maximum of at least 64 is recommended; composition recipes and routine periodic replacement are prohibited within this profile, with change required on compromise. Repository 16/20–128 settings address length; its static sample denylist does not establish adequate compromised-password coverage. [Authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/). | IAM/security: maintain the approved dataset, qualify all entry/change surfaces, and select any different legally required profile explicitly. Argon2 configuration alone proves neither NIST conformance nor FIPS validation. |
| Phishing resistance and sessions | AAL2 requires offering a phishing-resistant option; TOTP alone does not satisfy that requirement. Recommended AAL2 limits are 24 hours overall/one hour inactivity; AAL3 uses 12 hours/15 minutes. Shorter realm values do not establish an AAL. [Assurance levels](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/), [session management](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/session/). | IAM and client owners: select/qualify authenticators, reauthentication and actual relying-party sessions. Do not translate NIST AAL into eIDAS LoA or identity-proofing assurance automatically. |
| Recovery and lifecycle | Recovery differs by IAL/AAL. At AAL2, specified recovery-code combinations or repeated identity proofing, where available, are supported; recovery requires notification. New authenticator binding and compromised-authenticator invalidation have separate requirements. [Authenticator events](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/events/). | Identity assurance/operations: map the exact selected recovery method and loss scenario. Native known-password unlock is not proof that every lost-factor reproofing method meets the selected NIST profile. |

## Assurance, providers and legal-person authority

The selected architecture is **Core KYCB as KYC/KYB system of record, independent Domain adapters for third-party verification, and eIDAS as a design baseline**. This is an ownership decision. It does not designate a vendor, install biometric models or approve a legal scheme. The [system-of-record contract](https://github.com/fireflysoftwaresolutions/domain-customer-kyc-kyb/blob/42e8b558048f6f1d8b874db005ba9b2f1b45d709/docs/identity-system-of-record.md) distinguishes implemented records from future assurance/reference/mandate requirements.

| Assurance boundary | Current state | Required acceptance record and owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and evidence authority | Core persists cases, evidence references, bounded grants and audit; Domain authenticates provider evidence. Successful proofing in qualification uses a test-only signed provider excluded from production JARs. | Identity assurance/vendor owner: selected provider and trust profile; documentary authenticity, genuine live capture/matching where used, authoritative references, fraud controls, positive/negative outcomes and independent result verification. |
| Assurance level and provenance | No accepted provider LoA or complete structured assurance envelope is established. A stored `provider-id` or `policy-id` is not proof of the provider's assurance. | Define the scheme/profile/version, claimed and verified level, methods, issuer/verifier identity, timestamps/expiry, independent evidence provenance and authenticated result. State how each claim is validated; retain unknowns as unknown. |
| Evidence hash and custody | Evidence references exist; the future defined evidence-hash contract remains an acceptance item. | Data/assurance owners: define exactly which immutable bytes or canonical representation are hashed, algorithm, custody, provenance and verification. A hash establishes integrity of defined content, not that a document, person or vendor decision is truthful. |
| Existing customer linkage | Runtime grants bind exact stored subject/realm/tenant/party and purpose. Trusted production resolution of the existing reference identity remains required. | Customer-data/Core/Domain owners: approved resolver and mismatch handling. Never create or merge identities by name/email or turn an arbitrary provider success into ownership of an existing account. |
| Legal-person representatives | Institutional human authentication is separate from legal-person authority. No accepted representative-mandate validation is established. | Legal/KYB/authorization owners: current mandate source, represented entity, authorized person, role/scope, expiry/revocation, tenant binding and action-specific permission. Natural-person proofing cannot by itself authorize institutional transactions or BPO action. |
| Data processing and biometrics | No production biometric engine/provider acceptance exists; raw proofing artifacts do not belong in Keycloak profile attributes or JWTs. | Privacy/security/vendor owners: processing roles, legal bases/conditions, notices, retention, transfers, subprocessors, model/data licenses, error/bias/PAD testing where relevant and independent assurance. A WebAuthn device's local biometric unlock is a different boundary from server-side KYC biometric processing. |
| Separation of powers | Workload authentication and customer evidence are distinct; Core retrieves independently verified evidence before a bounded grant, and native execution rechecks current state. | Security/operations: production credentials, trust anchors, endpoint confinement, hold/contact/reference invalidation and independent approval. Service credentials, a support PIN, email possession or generic `approved=true` cannot supply missing human or business authority. |

## Incident and notification responsibilities

Maintain separate decision records and clocks. Notification of credential replacement to a previously verified address is a security control; it is not a regulator incident filing, a breach notice to affected individuals or PSD2 advance permanent-block warning. The native notification outbox and Core worker cannot make those legal determinations.

| Regime and source | Trigger and timing | Accountable owner / required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DORA / [2025/301 Article 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32025R0301)**; classify using the applicable [2024/1772 criteria](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2024/1772/oj). | For a major ICT-related incident, initial notification as early as possible, within four hours of major classification and normally no later than 24 hours after awareness. Late major classification has a separate four-hour rule. Intermediate report within 72 hours of initial notification; final within one month of the latest intermediate report. Assess specified exceptions and competent-authority procedures. | ICT incident/compliance owner: record awareness, classification and submissions separately; maintain current classification and reporting templates. An authentication failure alone is not automatically a reportable major incident. |
| **GDPR Articles 33–34** and [EDPB breach guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/assess-the-risks/data-breaches_en). | Controller informs the authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after awareness, unless unlikely to risk individuals' rights/freedoms. Processor informs controller without undue delay. High-risk affected-person communication is a separate duty. | Privacy/controller and incident owner: breach assessment, all required records, processor escalation and authority/individual decisions. DORA reporting does not replace this assessment. |

| **FTC 314.4(j)**, only where the rule applies. | Notify FTC as soon as possible, within 30 days of discovery of a qualifying notification event involving at least 500 consumers. Relevant unencrypted-information acquisition includes specified encryption-key compromise; access is presumed acquisition absent reliable contrary evidence. | Qualified Individual/privacy/incident owners: establish the exact trigger, affected count, discovery and notice. | **NYDFS 500.17**, for covered entities and qualifying incidents. | Notify as promptly as possible, within 72 hours after determining a cybersecurity incident occurred. | **Bank customer notice / state overlays.** [Interagency response-program guidance](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/guidance/interagency-guidance-on-response-programs-for-unauthorized-access-to-customer-information-and-customer-notice.htm). | Bank guidance calls for investigation and customer notice as soon as possible when sensitive customer information misuse occurred or is reasonably possible, subject to applicable delay provisions. Additional state/sector duties require their own scope and trigger analysis. | Legal/privacy/customer-communications owners: approved messages, recipients, channels, accessibility, permitted delays and records. Actual notification operations also need monitored worker availability, private metrics access, approved retry/escalation procedures, terminal-failure review, channel integrity and a data-retention owner. The worker is default-off, delivery is at least once and duplicates are possible after ambiguous SMTP acceptance. Do not delete queued evidence, reset counters or replay human proof to repair delivery.
