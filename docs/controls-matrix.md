# EU and US security and regulatory controls

**Reviewed November 1, 2026.** Use this guide to map the institution's users, services and data to the relevant EU and US requirements. The tables identify the configuration and operating work to perform, with primary sources for the legal/compliance team. Applicability depends on the entity and activity; the distribution carries no universal regulatory certification.

Start with the entity's charter/license, jurisdictions, customer and employee journeys, information processed and outsourced providers. Select authentication, recovery, privacy and resilience controls for those circumstances.

Use [security](security.md), [authentication policies](authentication-policies.md), [deployment setup](regulated-deployment.md) and [credential-loss recovery](credential-loss-recovery.md) alongside this guide.

## How to use the matrix

Record applicability before selecting the deployment profile. Assign named owners for the tasks below and keep the approved configuration, operating procedures and review dates in the institution's controlled records.

| Classification | Meaning in this page |
| --- | --- |
| Binding law or regulatory standard | An obligation only where its legal scope, effective date and relevant conditions apply. Directly applicable EU regulations differ from directives implemented through national law. |
| Supervisory guidance | Regulator-issued risk-management expectations or interpretation; distinguish these from a new statutory requirement. |
| Technical guidance or standard | A benchmark that may be adopted by policy, contract or a specific legal requirement; its publication alone does not make it law for every bank. |

### Applicability worksheet

Complete this worksheet for each deployment and material change. Store the approved record in the institution's controlled evidence system, without placing customer records or secrets in this public documentation.

| Decision | Required record | Accountable owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal entity and activity | Entity, license/charter, home/host jurisdiction, services, branches and primary/secondary supervisors | Legal and compliance |
| Applicable sources | Binding instruments, national implementation, effective dates, exemptions and documented rationale | Legal and compliance |
| Population and access | Customers, workforce, institutional humans, representatives, privileged administrators and workloads; actual resources accessed | Security and business owner |
| Journey | Ordinary sign-in, payment-account access, payment authorization, account maintenance, known-password unlock, credential replacement or representative action | Product and fraud owner |
| Information and locations | Personal/nonpublic data, evidence, credential material, subprocessors, processing/storage/support countries and transfers | Privacy and data owner |
| Required assurance | Identity-proofing and authentication targets, accepted scheme/provider evidence, freshness, factor binding and fallback procedure | Identity assurance and security |
| Authority | Existing customer/party linkage, tenant membership, legal-person mandate, employment and financial permissions, with their authoritative owners | Core/Domain and authorization owner |
| Policy exceptions | Exact exception, legal basis if applicable, compensating controls, approving authority, review and expiry | Compliance and security |
| Go-live review | Installed versions/configuration, application and provider checks, operating exercises and residual risks | Operations, security and independent assurance |

EU applicability must distinguish payment rules from general ICT resilience and privacy obligations. US applicability must distinguish bank-agency rules, FTC jurisdiction and state or sector overlays. Customer nationality, a hostname or a claimed vendor certification alone is not a sufficient applicability decision.

## Defaults to adapt

The [policy catalogue](../config/authentication-policies.json) is the source of these values. They are defaults for isolated synthetic realms. Apply changes to existing realms through supported administration/migration operations; import skips them.

| Population | Password and session defaults | MFA boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer humans | 16–128 characters; previous five excluded; 300-second SSO idle and access-token lifetime | Optional in the ordinary synthetic profile. Configure customer factors for the actual journey before rollout. |
| Workforce humans | 20–128 characters; previous ten excluded; 900-second SSO idle; 300-second access tokens | Browser password and OTP required; TOTP is the sandbox factor. |
| Institutional humans | 16–128 characters; previous five excluded; 300-second SSO idle and access-token lifetime | Optional in the synthetic profile; no organizational mandate or transaction authority inferred. |
| Administrators | 20–128 characters; previous ten excluded; 300-second idle, 3,600-second maximum and 60-second access tokens | Catalogue intent requires MFA; configure named administrators, phishing-resistant MFA and private administrative ingress, then remove temporary bootstrap access. |

All populations select Argon2, reject whole passwords equal to username/email, use a small static denylist, and permanently block after the third applicable failed authentication attempt. There is no automatic timed release. The baseline denylist is not comprehensive compromised-password coverage. No arbitrary periodic password expiry is selected. TOTP is not phishing resistant. The ordinary application-realm maximum is 28,800 seconds; none of these numbers is a universal banking-law recipe.

Identity SSO activity, application activity and payer activity are different measurements. Refresh traffic can keep an identity session active while the person is idle. Likewise, disabling an identity or retiring Keycloak sessions does not make every previously issued self-contained JWT immediately unusable at an independent receiver. See [receiver enforcement](zero-trust.md).

## European Union control mapping

| Source and scope | Control to assess | Configure and operate | Responsible team |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **PSD2 and SCA RTS — binding where the payment journey is in scope.** [Directive 2015/2366, Article 97](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2015/2366/oj) and [2018/389, Articles 4–9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02018R0389-20230912); apply permitted exemptions only after an explicit decision. | SCA for relevant account access, electronic payments and risky remote actions; factor protection/independence and dynamic linking where required. | Configure covered customer journeys with the required independent factors and transaction confirmation. Optional sandbox MFA does not supply amount/payee binding. | Payment-service and fraud owners: test actual payment journeys, exemptions, possession binding, transaction confirmation and dynamic linking. |
| **SCA RTS, Article 4 — binding within its scope.** | Generic element-failure information; blocking after at most five consecutive failures within a period; advance warning and secure restoration for permanent blocks. | The selected threshold is three, with permanent blocking and generic errors. Configure applicable advance warning and secure restoration separately. | Identity/product/compliance: review all applicable authenticators and an approved advance-warning channel. Removing a public lockout explanation does not discharge a notification obligation. Three failures is product policy, not the legislated universal number. |
| **SCA RTS, Article 4(3)(d).** [EBA account-inactivity interpretation](https://www.eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2018_4065) is explanatory guidance. | Maximum five minutes of actual payer inactivity after authentication for online payment-account access. | Customer identity idle is 300 seconds. Track payer activity in the account application, including refresh and background activity. | Payment/account application owner: enforce and test actual inactivity, refresh, background activity, reauthentication and independent application sessions. |
| **SCA RTS, Articles 23–27 — credential lifecycle.** | Secure creation, association, delivery, renewal/reactivation and revocation; remote association under 24(2)(b) uses SCA. | Native known-password unlock and customer credential replacement have distinct purpose-bound grants and execution checks. Configure the independent proofing adapter and existing-party reference before enabling credential replacement. | Identity assurance and payment owners: approve enrollment/reproofing and factor replacement, including lost-factor cases. Do not describe one password-plus-TOTP unlock recipe as the legally mandated method for every restoration. |
| **DORA — binding for covered entities.** [2022/2554, Articles 1–2, 5–16 and 28–30](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj?uri=CELEX%3A32022R2554), applicable from January 17, 2025, with entity-specific scope/exclusions. | Governed ICT risk, resilience, testing and third-party management. Determine full or simplified framework applicability. | Operate separate identity profiles and define recovery objectives, third-party responsibilities, continuity and resilience exercises for the selected topology. | Management body, CISO and ICT-risk owner: approve risk framework, dependency inventory, contracts, testing, exit and continuity evidence. |
| **DORA ICT RTS — binding in its applicable framework.** [2024/1774, Articles 20–21 and 33](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?qid=1719842051544&uri=CELEX%3A32024R1774). | Unique identities and lifecycle; least privilege; strong authentication for specified remote, privileged, critical/important and public ICT access. Article 21(e) includes at least annual access-right updates/reviews, or six-monthly for critical/important systems. | Separate realms/admin plane, controlled claims and required workforce OTP provide implementation components. Core/Domain relationships and application permissions remain independent authorities. | IAM and resource owners: cover joiner/mover/leaver, emergency access, access reviews, all privileged paths and nonhuman identities. Stronger password length alone does not meet these requirements. |
| **GDPR — binding when material and territorial scope applies.** [2016/679, Articles 3, 5–6, 9, 25, 28, 32, 35 and Chapter V](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679). | Lawful purpose, minimization, controlled retention, privacy by design, processor contracts, risk-appropriate security, DPIA where required and lawful transfers. | Minimize identity references and diagnostics. Select retention/transfer controls and assess the proposed proofing provider before sending documents or biometrics. | Privacy/data controller and security: approve data inventory, lawful basis, Article 9 condition where applicable, DPIA, processing agreements, residency/transfers and deletion/legal holds. A biometric comparison for unique identification needs a specific assessment; a photograph alone does not prove that processing occurs. |
| **eIDAS — binding for activities/schemes within scope; selected design baseline here.** [910/2014 as amended](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02014R0910-20241018) and [2015/1502 assurance criteria](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32015R1502). | Distinguish low/substantial/high electronic-identification assurance, proofing, authentication and lifecycle requirements; establish natural-person/legal-person linkage where relevant. | Core KYCB is selected as system of record and Domain as third-party integration owner. Choose and verify the applicable scheme, provider and assurance profile separately. | Identity assurance/legal owners: select the applicable trust profile and authenticate its evidence. Vendor `high`, a KYC status or a successful TOTP check cannot self-assign eIDAS qualification, legal-person authority or recovery permission. |
| **NIS2 / 2024/2690 — scoped legislation; ENISA — technical guidance.** [Implementing Regulation 2024/2690](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R2690) covers listed digital infrastructure/service/provider categories; [ENISA June 2025 guidance](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/nis2-technical-implementation-guidance) supports implementation. | Assess sector/entity applicability and DORA's sector-specific relationship under its Article 1(2). Annex 11.6.2(c) of 2024/2690 requires initial, predefined-interval and compromise-driven credential changes within its scope. | Baseline masking, length and blocklist controls map to parts of security hygiene. Baseline has no arbitrary expiry and no comprehensive breach dataset. | Compliance/security: select any legally required interval and maintained password dataset. ENISA advice is not a universal numerical EU banking password standard. |

The [EBA ICT/security guideline scope was narrowed after DORA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-amends-its-guidelines-ict-and-security-risk-management-measures-context-dora-application). Use the [May 2026 consolidated text](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-05/c8fb6168-0aef-42f6-8da9-114a8baf21b9/Consolidated%20version%20of%20EBA%20ameding%20Guidelines%20on%20ICT%20and%20security%20risk%20management.pdf) and competent-authority applicability/compliance records rather than treating the full 2019 text as unchanged. Track subsequent payment-law changes and their actual application dates; this page does not assume a successor proposal has displaced the cited operative controls.

### NIST technical assurance benchmark

[Final NIST SP 800-63B-4, July 2025](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/b/4/final) is a technical standard/guideline, with a government-system focus. It becomes an institution-specific requirement through an applicable mandate, contract or adopted policy; it is not a general private-bank MFA statute.

| Topic | Benchmark and configuration implications | Responsible team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passwords | NIST requires 15-character single-factor passwords, at least eight within MFA, full-value verification, and a common/expected/compromised-value blocklist. A maximum of at least 64 is recommended; composition recipes and routine periodic replacement are prohibited within this profile, with change required on compromise. Repository 16/20–128 settings address length; its static sample denylist does not establish adequate compromised-password coverage. [Authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/). | IAM/security: maintain the approved dataset, check all entry/change surfaces, and select any different legally required profile explicitly. Argon2 configuration alone proves neither NIST conformance nor FIPS validation. |
| Phishing resistance and sessions | AAL2 requires offering a phishing-resistant option; TOTP alone does not satisfy that requirement. Recommended AAL2 limits are 24 hours overall/one hour inactivity; AAL3 uses 12 hours/15 minutes. Shorter realm values do not establish an AAL. [Assurance levels](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/), [session management](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/session/). | IAM and client owners: select and test authenticators, reauthentication and actual relying-party sessions. Do not translate NIST AAL into eIDAS LoA or identity-proofing assurance automatically. |
| Recovery and lifecycle | Recovery differs by IAL/AAL. At AAL2, specified recovery-code combinations or repeated identity proofing, where available, are supported; recovery requires notification. New authenticator binding and compromised-authenticator invalidation have separate requirements. [Authenticator events](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/events/). | Identity assurance/operations: map the exact selected recovery method and loss scenario. Native known-password unlock is not proof that every lost-factor reproofing method meets the selected NIST profile. |

## Assurance, providers and legal-person authority

Core KYCB stores KYC/KYB records; customer Domain integrates the selected third-party verifier. eIDAS is the design baseline. Configure the actual provider, existing-party resolver and evidence policy using [the provider integration guide](assurance-provider-contract.md). No verifier or biometric engine is supplied.

| Area | Product behavior | Setup task and owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and evidence authority | Core persists cases, evidence references, bounded grants and audit; Domain authenticates provider evidence. A real provider adapter is required for production proofing. | Identity assurance/vendor owner: selected provider and trust profile; documentary authenticity, genuine live capture/matching where used, authoritative references, fraud controls, positive/negative outcomes and independent result verification. |
| Assurance level and provenance | Select the provider assurance profile and validate its provenance. A stored `provider-id` or `policy-id` does not establish assurance. | Define the scheme/profile/version, claimed and verified level, methods, issuer/verifier identity, timestamps/expiry, independent evidence provenance and authenticated result. State how each claim is validated; retain unknowns as unknown. |
| Evidence hash and custody | Evidence references are stored; specify and verify a canonical evidence representation and hash algorithm with the provider. | Data/assurance owners: define exactly which immutable bytes or canonical representation are hashed, algorithm, custody, provenance and verification. A hash establishes integrity of defined content, not that a document, person or vendor decision is truthful. |
| Existing customer linkage | Runtime grants bind exact stored subject/realm/tenant/party and purpose. Connect a trusted resolver for the existing reference identity. | Customer-data/Core/Domain owners: approved resolver and mismatch handling. Never create or merge identities by name/email or turn an arbitrary provider success into ownership of an existing account. |
| Legal-person representatives | Institutional human authentication is separate from legal-person authority. Connect current mandate validation through the owning business service. | Legal/KYB/authorization owners: current mandate source, represented entity, authorized person, role/scope, expiry/revocation, tenant binding and action-specific permission. Natural-person proofing cannot by itself authorize institutional transactions or BPO action. |
| Data processing and biometrics | Raw proofing artifacts belong with the designated provider/evidence owner, outside Keycloak profile attributes and JWTs. | Privacy/security/vendor owners: processing roles, legal bases/conditions, notices, retention, transfers, subprocessors, model/data licenses, error/bias/PAD testing where relevant and independent assurance. A WebAuthn device's local biometric unlock is a different boundary from server-side KYC biometric processing. |
| Separation of powers | Workload authentication and customer evidence are distinct; Core retrieves independently verified evidence before a bounded grant, and native execution rechecks current state. | Security/operations: production credentials, trust anchors, endpoint confinement, hold/contact/reference invalidation and independent approval. Service credentials, a support PIN, email possession or generic `approved=true` cannot supply missing human or business authority. |

## Incident and notification responsibilities

Maintain separate decision records and clocks. Notification of credential replacement to a previously verified address is a security control; it is not a regulator incident filing, a breach notice to affected individuals or PSD2 advance permanent-block warning. The native notification outbox and Core worker cannot make those legal determinations.

| Regime and source | Trigger and timing | Accountable owner / required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DORA / [2025/301 Article 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32025R0301)**; classify using the applicable [2024/1772 criteria](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2024/1772/oj). | For a major ICT-related incident, initial notification as early as possible, within four hours of major classification and normally no later than 24 hours after awareness. Late major classification has a separate four-hour rule. Intermediate report within 72 hours of initial notification; final within one month of the latest intermediate report. Assess specified exceptions and competent-authority procedures. | ICT incident/compliance owner: record awareness, classification and submissions separately; maintain current classification and reporting templates. An authentication failure alone is not automatically a reportable major incident. |
| **GDPR Articles 33–34** and [EDPB breach guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/assess-the-risks/data-breaches_en). | Controller informs the authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after awareness, unless unlikely to risk individuals' rights/freedoms. Processor informs controller without undue delay. High-risk affected-person communication is a separate duty. | Privacy/controller and incident owner: breach assessment, all required records, processor escalation and authority/individual decisions. DORA reporting does not replace this assessment. |
| **US bank-agency incident rules**; [FDIC final-rule explanation](https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2021/fil21074.html) and [Federal Reserve instructions](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/cybersecurity-and-operational-resilience.htm). | Bank notifies primary federal regulator as soon as possible, no later than 36 hours after determining a qualifying notification incident occurred. Service providers notify affected banks as soon as possible when the covered-services material-disruption criterion of four or more hours is met or reasonably likely. Four hours is a trigger, not a waiting period. | Bank incident owner and service-provider manager: determine materiality, record determination/notice, maintain designated contacts and contractual escalation. Select the bank's actual regulator. |
| **FTC 314.4(j)**, only where the rule applies. | Notify FTC as soon as possible, within 30 days of discovery of a qualifying notification event involving at least 500 consumers. Relevant unencrypted-information acquisition includes specified encryption-key compromise; access is presumed acquisition absent reliable contrary evidence. | Qualified Individual/privacy/incident owners: establish the exact trigger, affected count, discovery and notice. Do not apply this deadline as the universal US banking clock. |
| **NYDFS 500.17**, for covered entities and qualifying incidents. | Notify as promptly as possible, within 72 hours after determining a cybersecurity incident occurred. Extortion-payment notice within 24 hours; written explanation within 30 days. | Covered-entity incident/CISO/legal owners: assess entity/affiliate/provider events and rule-defined triggers, submissions and continuing updates. |
| **Bank customer notice / state overlays.** [Interagency response-program guidance](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/guidance/interagency-guidance-on-response-programs-for-unauthorized-access-to-customer-information-and-customer-notice.htm). | Bank guidance calls for investigation and customer notice as soon as possible when sensitive customer information misuse occurred or is reasonably possible, subject to applicable delay provisions. Additional state/sector duties require their own scope and trigger analysis. | Legal/privacy/customer-communications owners: approved messages, recipients, channels, accessibility, permitted delays and records. Prepare the required customer notice separately from routine account-recovery messages. |

Actual notification operations also need monitored worker availability, private metrics access, approved retry/escalation procedures, terminal-failure review, channel integrity and a data-retention owner. The worker is default-off, delivery is at least once and duplicates are possible after ambiguous SMTP acceptance. Do not delete queued evidence, reset counters or replay human proof to repair delivery.

## Maintain the institution profile

Review this mapping when the legal entity, services, jurisdictions, authenticators, recovery process, provider, processing location or runtime dependencies change. Keep exceptions time-bounded with an approving owner and review date.

Before go-live, complete the applicability worksheet; configure all population factor flows; verify provider/reference and representative authority; rehearse notifications, incidents and retained restore; and check authorization/revocation in the actual receiving applications. [Deployment setup](regulated-deployment.md) and [production settings](production-readiness.md) turn those decisions into operator tasks.
