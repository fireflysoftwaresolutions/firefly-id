# Permanent blocking and secure recovery

The selected policy permanently disables an account after three recorded authentication failures. It has no timed release. A password reset and an account unlock are different operations. The optional native two-factor known-password unlock and customer PIN lifecycle passed a fresh 48-gate real native/Core/Domain regression at the exact revisions in [current evidence](progress.md). The separate [credential-loss candidate](credential-loss-recovery.md) implements customer proofing and replacement without the old password, with independent qualification and external-provider gates. The ordinary theme-only distribution does not enable either native protocol. Read the [native installation and protocol](native-recovery.md) and current evidence before adoption. Production generation remains unavailable. Assisted administration requires its independently approved identity-reproofing procedure; a manage-user API permission alone does not establish that procedure.

## Exact native behavior

Keycloak 26.8.1 uses `bruteForceProtected=true`, `permanentLockout=true`, `failureFactor=3`, `maxTemporaryLockouts=0` and `quickLoginCheckMilliSeconds=0`. The direct permanent branch disables the user and sets `disabledReason=permanentLockout`. Waiting does not re-enable the account. This branch also skips the ordinary failure-age reset; retained wait/reset settings do not establish a timed unlocking window. A successful authentication before the block resets applicable failures. Other authenticators and distributed concurrent attempts require their own qualification. [Pinned implementation](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak/blob/26.8.1/services/src/main/java/org/keycloak/services/managers/DefaultBruteForceProtector.java).

The attack-detection DELETE operation removes a failure record, but does not itself re-enable the permanently disabled user. Conversely, an administrative user update with `enabled=true` is a privileged state change, with no native requirement for two-factor recovery proof. Do not expose either as a public recovery API. Never bulk-clear failures or enable an account merely because a caller knows its identifier. [Attack detection](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak/blob/26.8.1/services/src/main/java/org/keycloak/services/resources/admin/AttackDetectionResource.java), [administrative user update](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak/blob/26.8.1/services/src/main/java/org/keycloak/services/resources/admin/UserResource.java).

Stock password-reset selection treats disabled/unknown users generically, and ordinary action-token processing rejects disabled users. Do not temporarily enable an account to verify a factor. Password replacement must not clear the lock or an administrative/fraud/employment hold. A custom action-token handler alone cannot bypass the earlier disabled-user check safely. [Reset chooser](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak/blob/26.8.1/services/src/main/java/org/keycloak/authentication/authenticators/resetcred/ResetCredentialChooseUser.java), [action-token checks](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak/blob/26.8.1/services/src/main/java/org/keycloak/services/resources/LoginActionsServiceChecks.java).

## Regulatory basis and selected product policy

The consolidated PSD2 RTS, Delegated Regulation 2018/389 dated 12 September 2023, limits consecutive failed attempts to at most five in the relevant period, prohibits disclosure of the incorrect SCA element and requires advance warning before permanent blocking plus secure restoration. Three failures and no timed release are the owner's stricter selected defaults. The applicable bank must approve the restoration procedure and warning delivery. The owner requested removal of the explanatory lockout paragraph from both UI languages. An approved applicable payer-notification mechanism remains a separate production gate; this distribution does not claim satisfaction of that duty. [Articles 4 and 26](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02018R0389-20230912).

The selected known-password self-service policy requires **previously enrolled independent knowledge and possession evidence**. A password or separately enrolled recovery secret can provide knowledge when verified inside its credential boundary. Possession needs reliable proof of an enrolled device/key under the approved factor policy. An OTP received during recovery does not also count as knowledge; an email address or username is not a knowledge factor. An ordinary emailed reset link alone cannot unlock an account. Independence must be demonstrated even when both elements use the same device. These are engineering choices informed by the rules, not a claim that every European unlock has one identical legal factor prescription. Credential loss instead requires the independently approved alternative-evidence process described in the separate guide. [EBA SCA-elements Opinion, paragraphs 31–36](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/10180/2622242/4bf4e536-69a5-44a5-a685-de42e292ef78/EBA%20Opinion%20on%20SCA%20elements%20under%20PSD2%20.pdf), [EBA factor-independence answer](https://www.eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2019_4637).

## Intended journey and ownership

1. Qualify the applicable bank-approved advance notification mechanism separately. Sign-in errors, recovery initiation and recovery failures use generic responses, without existence/status, factor-error details or remaining counts. Apply bounded abuse controls to sign-in and recovery; otherwise an attacker can deliberately lock another person's account.
2. Create a recovery-only transaction with unpredictable server-side state and CSRF/session binding. It is not an authenticated user session and cannot issue OAuth codes or tokens. Bind the candidate privately to the exact issuer, population, opaque tenant, subject, intended client/purpose and authoritative lock generation. Unknown/ineligible candidates must not acquire a distinguishable public eligibility response.
3. Verify the pre-existing knowledge element inside Keycloak when Keycloak owns that credential. Verify possession through its already enrolled trusted credential channel. Raw passwords, PINs, OTPs, private keys and biometrics never become Domain/Core business DTOs, logs or recovery-proof claims. A new factor cannot be enrolled inside this flow before recovery has been authorized.
4. Verify current proof freshness, independent factor categories, nonce, audience, subject, purpose and lock generation. Enforce expiry, bounded attempts and atomic single-use consumption. Reject replay, cross-account/realm/tenant/client substitution and proofs belonging to an earlier block. Current actor authorization and applicable PDP obligations remain mandatory at each service boundary.
5. Atomically authorize the exact eligible lock transition, consume its recovery transaction and record the decision. An administrative/fraud/employment hold or changed lock generation must prevent enablement. Stock read-then-write Admin REST does not provide this compare-and-set contract. Read-only/federated stores without equivalent guarantees must refuse the operation. Do not mistake local proof consumption for atomic completion of a remote provider mutation.
6. Require fresh ordinary sign-in and applicable MFA after recovery. Retire affected sessions according to the approved policy. Recovery does not restore old sessions, create grants, change identity links or confer financial authority.

The optional Keycloak `RealmResourceProvider` owns recovery-only credential verification and the native transition, with version-specific provider qualification. Existing disabled-user authentication/action-token handling is not reused as ordinary login success. Native source compilation/tests and integrated runtime acceptance remain separate facts. [Supported custom REST extension point](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak/blob/26.8.1/docs/documentation/server_development/topics/extensions.adoc).

The genuine Core SCA source candidate persists protected challenges, committed verification reservations and recovery evidence; Domain orchestrates through its actual original-producer SDK and owns no credentials or business persistence. Those new recovery-specific APIs are separate from ordinary challenge CRUD/status. They require coordinated native integration and do not make legacy status updates an unlock permission. An authenticated machine call is not customer proof. Jafly remains independent: shared interfaces model product-neutral verification/current-state admission, with no Firefly Identity branding or Keycloak-specific recovery behavior coupled into the framework.

## Population and assisted recovery

Customer, workforce and institutional-human recovery remain issuer/population scoped. The password-only customer sample has no qualified enrolled possession factor and is not eligible for automatic two-factor recovery. Workforce factors cannot recover a customer or master administrator. Master administrators use a separate privileged recovery plane, approved strong factors, independent authorization and emergency-access controls.

Forgotten knowledge, lost/revoked possession, suspected takeover and inconsistent enrollment require bank-approved assisted identity reproofing, with scoped operator authority, independent approval as required by the approved risk policy, audit, secure re-enrollment and notifications. Support must not request the old secret, substitute caller ID/account history for proof, or enroll a replacement factor using only an email. Manual disabling and fraud/HR holds remain outside self-service unlock.

## Full-platform blocking and acceptance gates

Native permanent blocking does not revoke every existing session or invalidate an issued self-contained JWT. Refresh paths that check the disabled user refuse it, but an offline decoder can still accept an unexpired JWT. Full blocking requires explicit session/refresh/application-session handling and receiver enforcement of current identity state. Short token lifetimes bound exposure; they do not establish immediate revocation. The shared framework current-state boundary is a separate integration gate. [Native token validation](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak/blob/26.8.1/services/src/main/java/org/keycloak/protocol/oidc/TokenManager.java).

Installed/provider evidence must establish exact third-failure disabling; generic presentation; refusal with the correct credential, cleared failure counter, password replacement and old reset link; persistence across restart/time advance; existing-session and refresh behavior; receiver refusal after blocking; single-use concurrent recovery; hold/generation races; crash/retry semantics; factor independence and accessible branded customer/employee/operator journeys. The current HTTP synthetic sandbox cannot qualify WebAuthn on `id.firefly.test`; real HTTPS and trusted test certificates are needed. Unsafe-origin browser flags or skipped TLS validation do not qualify possession proof.

The policy runner implements isolated-provider assertions for the permanent block and separate reset behavior. A written runner, source tests, a design or administrative readback is not a successful runtime result. The optional known-password unlock has scoped actual Keycloak/Core/Domain acceptance. The credential-loss candidate has its own source and synthetic-provider evidence; real proofing, full-platform block enforcement and regulatory/production acceptance remain explicit pending gates.
